\subsection*{Task: Support call center 1: [2.2025]}

In a user support center, calls from users arrive at an average rate of $3$ calls per hour. It is assumed that the number of calls arriving in one hour follows a $Poisson$ distribution $P(3)$. To improve the user experience, the support center has implemented the following policy:

\begin{itemize}
    \item If at most $2$ calls arrive in an hour, no alarm state is declared.

\item If $3$ or $4$ calls arrive in an hour, an alarm state is declared if at least $2$ of the calls contain a complaint.

\item If at least $5$ calls arrive in an hour, an alarm state is declared.

\end{itemize}

A call contains a complaint in $5\%$ of cases, independently of the content of other calls.

\begin{itemize}
    \item What is the probability that the support center declares an alarm state?
    \item If the support center declared an alarm state, what is the probability that exactly $4$ calls arrived in one hour?
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Task: Game [11.06.2024]}

The friends Petar, Martin, and Lena are playing a game with 2 red, 2 blue, 2 green, and 4 white shells. There can be multiple winners in the game. Without looking, they choose 3 shells so that each of them picks one (they choose the shells at once).

\begin{itemize}
    \item If among the chosen shells there is at least one red, then Petar is a winner.

    \item If among the chosen shells there is at least one blue, then Martin is a winner.

    \item If among the chosen shells there is at least one green, then Lena is a winner.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
    \item [a)] What is the probability that at least one of the three will not win?

    \item [b)] Vlado is angry at the three friends because they are playing in his yard. He throws a fair die:

\begin{itemize}
    \item If it lands on number 1, he kicks out Petar.

    \item If it lands on number 2, he kicks out Martin.

    \item If it lands on number 3, he kicks out Lena.

    \item If it lands on number 4, he randomly chooses two of them to kick out.

    \item If it lands on 5 or 6, he kicks out all three of them.
\end{itemize}

Meanwhile, after the game in part $A)$, they all returned the shells. But Martin ate one shell, and it is not known which one. After this change (with one shell eaten), all three repeat the game, and then Vlado kicks out at least one of them according to the rule above.

What is the probability that Martin wins and still gets kicked out of the yard?
\end{itemize}
